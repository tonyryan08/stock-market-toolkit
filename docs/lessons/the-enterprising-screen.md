# The enterprising screen — tutor notes

**Phase 2 · a segment for lesson 5 or 6 (25 minutes) · grounding for the paper portfolio in weeks 8–9**

*What it is.* `/screen/enterprising` runs Graham's second checklist over the same books as the defensive screen. Its source is *The Intelligent Investor*, ch. 15, "A Winnowing of the Stock Guide" (course copy pp. 385–386). The printable version is page 2 of `/card`.

## The six requirements, and what changed from ch. 14

| # | Enterprising (ch. 15) | Defensive (ch. 14) | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P/E ≤ 9 on the latest year | P/E ≤ 15 on the 3-year average | **stricter**, and applied first |
| 2 | Current ratio ≥ 1.5; debt ≤ 110% of working capital | ≥ 2; ≤ 100% | relaxed |
| 3 | No deficit in 5 years | Earnings in each of 10 years | relaxed |
| 4 | Some current dividend | 20 years uninterrupted | relaxed |
| 5 | Latest EPS above four fiscal years earlier (the oldest of the five years held — Graham's 1970 v 1966) | +⅓ over ten years | relaxed |
| 6 | Price < 120% of **net tangible** assets | P/B ≤ 1.5 (or P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5) | **stricter**: goodwill doesn't count |
| — | **No size test** (p. 386) | Sales ≥ $100m (1971) | removed |

The point to make in one sentence: **the enterprising investor relaxes on quality and record, and tightens on price.** The extra effort pays for reading companies that the defensive tests would reject, but only at bargain prices.

## Graham's definitions (put them on the board)

- Defensive: wants "freedom from effort, annoyance, and the need for making frequent decisions" (p. 6).
- Enterprising: "willingness to devote time and care" (p. 6). This is about effort, not nerve, and the course has decided not to call it "risk appetite."
- Small companies "may afford enough safety if bought carefully and on a group basis" (p. 386).
- The screen gives a list, not a choice. From about 150 survivors Graham expected "a third selection of, say, one out of five" by judgment (p. 386). That is why the page says **candidate**, not survivor.

## How the toolkit adapts it (openly)

- **Test 1** uses the latest *fiscal* year's diluted EPS, because the Stock Guide's latest-12-months figure is not held. This is the only test marked *adapted* on the card.
- **"Debt"** is read as long-term debt, the figure held.
- **"Some current dividend"** means an ex-date within the last 12 months.
- **Financial companies skip test 2** (Graham's parenthesis: "for industrial companies") and are scored out of 5.
- **Tangible book** is equity minus goodwill and intangibles, per diluted share. It fills as the accounts rotation refetches each company after v0.5.3; until then the column shows "?".

## The worked example: Ethan Allen (ETD), 14 Oct 2026

ETD was the test company for VC-13/14. It is a small, dull furniture maker that FMP's free plan won't serve. From its FY2026 10-K at the 14 Oct close of $20.06:

- **P/E** 20.06 ÷ 1.56 = **12.9 → fails test 1.** Cheap by defensive standards, not cheap enough for Graham's enterprising cut.
- **Strength** CR 305.5 ÷ 148.4 = 2.06, no debt → passes. **Stability** passes. **Dividend** current → passes.
- **Growth** FY2026 1.56 vs FY2022 4.05 (EDGAR) → **fails**. Earnings have fallen since the post-Covid peak.
- **Tangible book** (471.5 − 45.1)m ÷ 25.58m = $16.66, so price is **1.20× → fails** "less than 120%" by a whisker.

So ETD fails three tests (1, 5 and 6) and passes three, which makes it **not a candidate**. It is still a strong teaching case: a strong balance sheet, a real dividend, and earnings that peaked and are falling. Ask the room: *is the market wrong about ETD, or right?* (The figures will move; recompute on the day from the books page.)
