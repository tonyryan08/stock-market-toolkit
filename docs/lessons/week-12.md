# Lesson 12 — Demo Day (one decision each, judged on process; the door, the kit, and the course's own retrospective)

**90 minutes · Phase 4, week 3 of 3 · Toolkit feature: the real student logins — the whole site behind Cloudflare Access (an email one-time code at the door, only the enrolled addresses on the list), the Worker handed the signed-in email and matching it to the student's row: `/me` is the student's page, found by their email; a code page opens for its own student and the tutor only. The takeaway kit assembled (`/kit`, `kit/`); the repository tagged v1.0 with a README a stranger could follow.**

*Methodology: the course's rubric turned on the students and then on the course itself. A decision is presented and questioned on five points — what the rule said, how many and why, where the stop was, what was written down, whether it was done — and the sixth question, what the price did, is not asked. The same five are asked of the twelve weeks, and the open-items register is read aloud as the honest answer to the fifth. Decision #23 in the syllabus.*

Phase 4 is the takeaway. Tonight nothing new is taught: the room does what the course has trained it to do, in public, and leaves with the kit. The toolkit grows its last feature — the door — and the course closes its own record the way it has closed every week, on evidence.

## By the end, students can

1. Present one decision from their own journal in four minutes on the five questions of the rubric, and answer the room's questions on process without reaching for the result.
2. Sign in to the course site with the one-time code sent to their email, find their own page at `/me`, and say what changed (the address is no longer the key) and what did not (everything else).
3. Say where every document of the kit is (`/kit`, the repository's `kit/` folder), which two to print, and what the Monday routine is once the course has ended.
4. Judge the course by its own rubric — including the open items it did not finish — and say what that honesty is for.

## Materials

- The site behind the door, switched on the week before (tutor notes): each student's email on their row (`npm run enrol -- --list` shows it) and on the Access policy (the dashboard's); the tutor signed in on the projector machine.
- Each student's own review page, reached through `/me` → "review my process", with the audit's lines — the referee for the presentations.
- **Demo day — the rubric and the retrospective** (`kit/demo-day.md`) printed, one each; the rules card and the journal template printed for anyone who lost theirs.
- `/kit` open in a tab; the repository's README open in another (the "How this was built" section, for the retrospective).
- A timer the room can see: four minutes a presentation, three questions, three sentences.

## Running order

**0:00 — Harvest, and the door (10 min).**
Round the room one last time: the Monday line. Then the door, on the projector: open the course address, sign out if signed in (the landing's "sign out" link — `/cdn-cgi/access/logout`), and show the room what a student sees — Cloudflare's page, the email, the code arriving, the site. `/me`: the page found by the email, no code typed. Say what changed in one sentence — *your email is the key now; the page's address no longer is* — and what did not: every tool is where it was, the paper account is the same account, the codes are still how the tutor refers to an account. Say what the toolkit does with the email: matches it to the row, and nothing else (the kit's quickstart says the same). The syllabus's exit check for the phase, read aloud: *the Access policy admits only enrolled emails* — and show it: a browser with an address that is not on the list is told "a code has been emailed" and no code ever comes — the refusal looks like silence, by Cloudflare's design (tutor notes: the rehearsal).

**0:10 — Demo day, first half (25 min).**
The rubric on the projector (`kit/demo-day.md`, section 2) and in every hand. Five presentations of four minutes each, the audit's lines for the account on the projector beside the presenter: *what did the rule say · how many and why · where was the stop and what was the plan · what did you write down and when · did you do what you wrote.* After each, three questions from the room on process (section 3) and three sentences (section 4) — one thing the process did well, one thing the journal could have carried, one thing the room learned — written into the presenter's journal. The tutor's one job is the sixth question: when anyone, presenter or audience, starts on what the price did, say "that is luck's; question five again". Keep the timer honest.

**0:35 — Demo day, second half (25 min).**
Five more. Vary the order so that a position that went against its owner and was closed on the stop sits beside one that is still open and was never tested: the room should hear, by the end, that the two are graded the same way and that the first is the better exhibit. If a presenter's audit line says *Not followed*, that is the best four minutes of the evening — the question is what the journal says about it and what was done after, not whether it cost anything.

**1:00 — The course's own retrospective (15 min).**
The rubric turned on the course (section 5), the tutor presenting and the room questioning. *What did the rules say?* Graham for what, Carver for when and how much — written into a toolkit that enforces them; where a human was still needed (the quarantine, the unknown split, the unlinked account). *How much, and why?* Paper money, $10,000, a 12% target. *Where was the stop?* The course's checks — the explainer's and the tutor's grounding, the review's withheld readings, the independent review before every shipment, the live check after; what they caught, and what slipped through, with the record's names for each (the leaked code of week 8 kept as an exhibit; the seed shipped stale and caught by the reviewer; the "is well" false positive caught by the first live rejection). *What was written down?* The build log, the syllabus's decisions, the register, the record of live checks, all in the open repository — the README's "How this was built" section on the projector. *Did the course do what it wrote?* The register's open items read aloud, every one: the Bill in December, the broker's figures, the audit copy waiting for its first live rejection, whatever else is open the night of the lesson. A course that ends with an honest list of what it did not finish has followed its own rules; say so, and mean it.

**1:15 — The kit, handed over, and the toolkit's future (10 min).**
`/kit` on the projector: the seven documents, what each is for, where each lives. The two to print; the links for the rest. Then the toolkit: it stays up behind the door for as long as the tutor says tonight (decision #23 c — the register records the date), the nightly jobs running on, the paper accounts growing their records; every line of it in the open repository for anyone who wants to run their own, with a README written for exactly that stranger. The Monday routine after the course (section 7): five minutes a week, the review page once a week, the costs-and-tax page once a year against that year's Finance Act. The course ends; the routine does not.

**1:25 — Close (5 min).**
No homework. The three promises, read by three students: paper portfolios only; no tips, ever; everything is open. Then the sentence, one last time, by the room.

## Tutor notes

- **The door, the week before.** Three things in order, and the order matters: (1) every student's email on their row — `npm run enrol -- --set-email CODE EMAIL` for each account enrolled before week 12; `--list` shows which still lack one; (2) the same addresses, and your own, on the Access policy (Zero Trust → Access controls → Policies; the Include rule is **Emails**, never *Email domain*, which would admit every address at a domain); (3) only then the switch — Workers & Pages → the Worker → **Access** → *Protect this Worker behind Access* → *All traffic* → the policy → *Apply Access*. The secret `TUTOR_EMAILS` (your address, or addresses, comma-separated: `npx wrangler secret put TUTOR_EMAILS`) before the switch too, or your own code pages close to you. The setup guide's Part E has the steps with the screens named; the record's VC-24 is the rehearsal.
- **Signing out, for the demo.** Cloudflare serves `/cdn-cgi/access/logout` on every Access-protected address: the landing's signed-in line links it ("sign out"), and the address bar works too. The session otherwise lasts what E4 set; a private window is the other way to a fresh door.
- **The rehearsal.** Sign in as yourself: the landing says who you are; every code page opens, each saying "signed in as … (the tutor)". Sign in as a student (a second browser, their address): `/me` lands on their page; another student's code answers 403 with the way to their own; the review button works; the tutor's own page is not theirs to open. An address not on the policy: "A code has been emailed to you" and nothing arrives — the door, and no further; that is the phase's exit check, and the silence is Cloudflare's design (a blocked address is not told it is blocked). Then the API from a signed-in page (the same cookie): `/api/me` names you; the signals and the screen answer as before. The nightly jobs are unaffected — Access gates requests, not the schedule; the receipt the next morning has its six rows.
- **Two accounts on one email.** The tutor's test accounts may share your address (`--allow-shared-email`): `/me` then shows a page of doors rather than a redirect, and each code page opens. A student's address links one account; the enrol script refuses a second.
- **What a student sees on day one.** Cloudflare's page asks for the email and *Send login code*; the PIN arrives by email and lives ten minutes; *Sign in*. The session lasts the application's session duration (24 hours unless you set it longer when you apply Access — the dialog shows it). A wrong address — a second email the student uses, say — is the commonest failure and looks like silence (no code comes): the fix is the policy and the row, both. The One-time PIN login method must have been added in Zero Trust (setup guide E3): a new organisation's default is Cloudflare's own identity provider, not the email code.
- **If the door must come off** (a policy mistake on the night, say): the Worker's Access tab again — remove the policy — and the Worker falls back to the code scheme at once, nothing redeployed, `/me` saying so. The code is the key again until the door goes back on; say that to the room if it happens.
- **The presentations.** Ten students is the class; with more, the second half shortens to three minutes each and the questions to two. A student who did not write a ticket presents the decision not to — the rule flat all term, the journal's "waiting" lines, and question five is whether they waited by the rules.
- **The retrospective's honesty.** Read the register's open items as they stand on the night, not a tidied version; the exhibits named in the running order are in the record (`docs/data-provider-checks.md`: VC-20 for the code, the v0.13.0 review's F2 for the seed, the VC-23 fix4 closure for the false positive). If a student asks why a course would show its own faults, the answer is week 6's: opacity is where the fee-takers live, and a course that hid its register would be asking for the trust it told the room never to give.
- **The tag.** After the lesson, the repository is tagged v1.0 by the tutor (GitHub Desktop → the commit → *Create tag* → `v1.0.0`, then push the tag); the README's state line and the register say so. The tag marks the course as taught, not the toolkit as finished: the register stays open, and the Bill's December figures are its first post-course entry.
