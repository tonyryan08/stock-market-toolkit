# Lesson 11 — The Real World (a broker's mechanics, what a trade costs, what the gain is taxed at — and did you follow your rules?)

**90 minutes · Phase 4, week 2 of 3 · Toolkit feature: explain-my-portfolio — the process review (`/tickets/<code>/review`, `GET /api/explain-my-portfolio/<code>`): every ticket and position read against the rules the student chose, by an audit that needs no AI and a reading by the course's AI from a sheet that carries no P&L; and the costs-and-tax worksheet (`/costs`, `GET /api/costs`): the same paper trade priced in euro the way an Irish student would pay for it, the gain taxed three ways.**

*Methodology: the course's grounding discipline (weeks 6 and 10) turned on the student's own book — a numbered sheet of process facts, a reading that may say only what is on it, checks that flag what is not, and a new check for outcome language, so that "well done, it went up" cannot be said even by accident. The tax walkthrough is education: every rate with its source and its date, the Bill marked as a Bill, and nothing in it advice for anyone's own case. Decision #22 in the syllabus.*

Phase 4 is the takeaway. Last week the tutor; tonight the real world — what the paper account would have cost in a real one, what the gain would be taxed at, how to read a broker's screen and a regulator's register, and a review of eleven weeks of decisions that looks at the decisions and not at the results. The point of the evening is that the course's process survives contact with the real world: the ticket's cost line was a real cost, the journal entry is the record the tax form will one day need, and a rule you followed is still the thing to grade, whatever the price did.

## By the end, students can

1. Read a broker's fee schedule and a trade confirmation and say what the trade cost — commission, handling, conversion, spread — and compare it with the course's model ($2 + 0.25%), using `/costs`.
2. Say how the same gain is taxed as shares, as a fund or ETF, and inside the investment account the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 would open — the rate, the exemption or its absence, the dates — and say which of those figures are law tonight and which are a Bill.
3. Check whether a firm is authorised on the Central Bank's register, say what a clone firm is, and name the protection lost when dealing with an unauthorised one.
4. Read the process review of a paper account — the audit's "not followed" lines and the AI's reading — and check every fact tag against the sheet, as with the tutor.

## Running order

**0:00 — Harvest: the Monday line, and a question nobody can answer yet (10 min).**
Round the room, the Monday line from the journal: the rule's state, the stop, the report's sentence, the watchlist's word. Then the question: *what has your paper account cost you?* Everyone has a number on their page — the ticket's cost line — and nobody knows what it stands for. Tonight it gets a receipt. Say the frame once: the course has taught *what* (Graham), *when and how much* (Carver), and *how to check* (the tutor); the real world adds *what it costs* and *what you keep*. Neither changes the rules.

**0:10 — A broker's mechanics, in the course's words (15 min).**
Project the broker's own fee page (DEGIRO's, the broker named in week 1; any MiFID broker on an EU register works the same way and the numbers differ). Seven things to read off it, each one sentence:
- **Who regulates it.** DEGIRO is a branch of a German bank, supervised by BaFin, serving Ireland under the EU passport under Dutch conduct rules — it is on the German and Dutch regulators' registers and is not supervised by the Central Bank of Ireland (the Bank's register is where an Irish-authorised firm appears; a passported firm is checked on its home regulator's). The habit (1:15 tonight): find the firm on a regulator's register *before* the money moves, and use the register's contact details, not the ones you were given.
- **The profile.** A "Basic" profile — no margin, no short selling, no derivatives — is the only one the course would recognise: the Starter System's unleveraged variant, leverage factor 1 (week 8). The other profiles exist to lend you money; the course has said why that is out of scope.
- **The order.** Market (at best), limit (your price or better; it may not fill), stop-loss and stop-limit (triggered intraday), with a validity of a day or until cancelled. Say the course's difference out loud: **the course's stop is a closing-price rule** — read on the close, the ticket written the next day — not an order resting with the broker that fires on a wobble at 11am. Both are "stops"; they behave differently, and the journal should say which you mean.
- **The currency.** A euro account buying a US share converts to dollars: automatically at 0.25% each way, or by a manual conversion with a fixed fee plus the 0.25%. The dollar balance then sits in the account; the dividend arrives in dollars. The course's paper account is in dollars because the shares are (lesson 8) — the worksheet converts the costs to euro so the room can feel them.
- **The dividend.** A US dividend is withheld at 30% unless the broker holds your W-8BEN, which brings it to 15% under the Ireland–US treaty — the first dividend statement tells you which you got. The rest is income (0:40).
- **The small print.** Securities lending (the broker may lend your shares; the clause says whether and what you get), the exchange connectivity fee (€2.50 a year for a foreign exchange), the compensation scheme (German: deposits to €100,000, investments 90% to €20,000) — read as facts, not fears.
- **The statement.** An annual statement of transactions is raw data: buys, sells, distributions, conversions. The broker reports nothing to Revenue for you. Your record is your own — which is exactly what the ticket and the journal have been for eleven weeks.

**0:25 — What the trade really costs (15 min).**
`/costs` on the projector with the defaults: €10,000 into a US share for three years, sold with a 30% gain. Read the trade's costs line by line — two orders at €2, conversion both ways (€25 and €32.50), the spread (week 2's toll bridge, €10 at 0.1%), three years of connectivity (€7.50): **€79** to open and close. Beside it the course's own model: $2 + 0.25% a ticket, both tickets, **$61.50** — the paper account's cost line was a real cost, give or take the conversion. Then the ETF column: €1 an order on a euro venue, no conversion, and the annual charge taken inside the price (0.2% a year, €69 over the holding — the cost you never see on a statement). The sentence to land: *the trade costs tens of euro; the holding costs tens a year; the tax, in a minute, costs hundreds.* Change the inputs live — a €2,000 trade, a 1% spread on a small cap (week 2's surprise) — and watch the order change.

**0:40 — Irish tax, the walkthrough (20 min).**
Three regimes, the kit page in every pair's hands, the worksheet's three cards on the projector. Every figure with its source and its date; the Bill marked as a Bill.
- **Shares, outside any account — capital gains tax.** 31% of the gain, for disposals on or after 7 October 2026 (Budget 2027; 33% before). The first €1,270 of a year's gains is exempt. A loss on shares offsets a gain on shares and carries forward (the four-week rule: a loss on shares bought back within four weeks is restricted). Pay by **15 December** for disposals from January to November, by **31 January** for December's; the return — CG1, or Form 11 / Form 12 — by **31 October** of the following year. The worksheet: €3,000 gain → €1,730 taxable → **€536.30**.
- **A fund or ETF, outside any account — the exit tax.** 38% of the gain or distribution in 2026 (41% to the end of 2025; Budget 2026 cut it), **35% from 1 January 2027** as announced in Budget 2027 and drafted in the Bill. No exemption; a loss on one fund cannot be set against anything; a **deemed disposal every eight years** taxes the gain as if sold (credited at the real sale). The worksheet: **€1,050** on the same €3,000, €1,140 if sold this year. Self-assessed on Form 11 / Form 12 — the broker's statement is raw data.
- **Inside the Individual Investment Account** — the Bill's name; "Personal Investment Account" in the Budget speech. From **1 July 2027** on a commencement order: 18 or over, resident, one account; **€12,000** a year in; **nothing** inside — no CGT, no dividend withholding, no exit tax, no deemed disposal — and instead **1% a year on the average daily value above €50,000**, calculated by the provider each 31 December and deducted from the account (a charge on value: payable in a losing year too). The worksheet: **€0** on a €10,000 trade; €570 over three years on a €60,000 one. Two things the Bill as published does not settle, and the room should hear it: whether a share listed only in the US qualifies, and how US withholding is treated inside. Both are for the Act and Revenue's guidance.
- **Dividends** are income: US dividends withheld at 15% (with the W-8BEN) and then taxed at your marginal rate with USC and PRSI, with credit for the 15%; an Irish company's dividend has 25% withholding tax taken and credited. The worksheet shows the withholding and stops there — the rest depends on the person.
- **Stamp duty**: 1% buying shares in an Irish company, 0.5% UK, none on US shares or Irish-domiciled ETFs — at purchase, on the worksheet's Irish column.
- **The discipline.** The Bill was published on 19 October 2026; it is expected to be an Act in December; the account needs a commencement order. The page says "as checked on 12 November 2026" and marks every line that depends on the Bill. Before anyone acts on a figure, it is re-checked against the Finance Act and Revenue's guidance — the course models the check, it does not give the advice. **Record-keeping** closes the segment: date, quantity, price, the conversion rate, the cost line, for every buy and sell — the ticket has had all of them since week 8; a journal is a tax record with a reason attached.

**1:00 — Explain my portfolio: did you follow your rules? (15 min).**
The test account's review page on the projector. Two parts, in order:
- **The audit** — the rules applied to the rows, no AI: *Not followed* (a stop hit and the position still open more than a trading week later; a close with the stop not hit; a close later than the week's routine), *Followed* (the stop answered within the week; the position opened with the rule long), *Journal* (word tests — the entry names no rule, no stop; said as word tests), *The report* (the flags, counted — and a drawdown is not a breach). Read the test account's lines aloud.
- **The reading** — press the button: the course's AI writes two or three paragraphs from a numbered sheet of **process facts only**: tickets, dates, the rule's state, whether the stop was hit, the journal's words, the flags. **The sheet carries none of the toolkit's profit, loss, account-value or drawdown figures and no price level beyond the entry** — open "The sheet the AI reads" under the page and show the room there is no P&L on it. It cannot praise what it cannot see. Say the one thing it can see: *your own journal words* — if an entry says "made €500", that is on the sheet as you wrote it; the AI is told to quote entries in fragments only and to repeat no result words, and the net is partial by nature. Then the checks: a fact tag not on the sheet, a paragraph that cites nothing, a number from nowhere, a tip, and — new tonight — outcome language ("well done", "profit", "it went up", "lucky") — **any one of them and the reading is withheld**, the audit standing on its own. The page says why.
- **The exercise**, pairs, five minutes: the printed fixture from the suite — three positions, one profitable close with the stop not hit. *What does the audit say about KO?* (Not followed: closed with the stop not hit; the journal says "nervous".) *What would a reading that praised the profit be?* (Withheld.) The syllabus's exit check for the week is exactly this: a deliberately rule-breaking test book is reviewed and the breach is flagged, not the P&L — pinned in `src/lib/review.test.ts`, shown live on the test account (VC-23).
- **The habit.** Monday evening, five minutes, as before — with the review page once a week. Three readings a day per account, the class's budget on the course's key; the audit is free and always there (the page and the API's plain address show it with no AI call; only the button, or `?ai=1` on the API, asks for a reading — so a link pasted into a chat spends nothing). The button lands you back on the page with the reading: a refresh never spends a second one.

**1:15 — Scam literacy II: platforms (10 min).**
Week 6 was stories versus numbers; tonight is the platform itself. The Central Bank's register on the projector: type a firm's name; read the entry; **call the number on the register, not the number on the website**. A clone firm copies a real firm's name, address and authorisation number and changes only the bank account — the Bank publishes a warning most weeks; read tonight's. Then the list, one breath each, the kit page in their hands: an unregistered or "offshore-regulated" platform; guaranteed or "fixed" returns; pressure and a deadline; a celebrity or an ad that found you; remote-access software "to help you set up"; a fee to withdraw; a second firm that will "recover your losses" for a fee; crypto or forex "platforms" with a dashboard that only ever goes up. What you lose with an unauthorised firm: the Investor Compensation Scheme and the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman — both exist only for authorised firms. Report to the Bank: (01) 224 5800, centralbank.ie/financialscams (both as printed on the Bank's own warning notices — checked at the build; check again before printing). The link to week 10: the confident wrong answer and the confident wrong platform sell the same way — fluency. The defence is the same: find the register entry, find the number, check it yourself.

**1:25 — Homework + close (5 min).**
1. **Price your own book.** Run `/costs` with your largest paper trade's size and its gain or loss to date; write the three tax figures in the journal, with the date you checked them and the line "Bill, not law" where it applies.
2. **Ask for a reading** of your account and check two fact tags against the sheet; bring one flag if you got one.
3. **The register check**, written into the journal as a rule: *before any money moves, the firm on a register, the number from the register.*
4. **Demo day**: choose the one decision from your journal you will present next week — judged on process, not on what the price did. Bring the entry, before and after.

Close with the sentence.

## Tutor notes

- **Pre-check the evening before:** `/costs` renders with the defaults and the "As checked on 2026-11-12" status line; the register page opens; the test account's review page shows the audit and one reading (ask for it the evening before — `review_ok` in the receipts: `SELECT run_at, outcome, detail FROM fetch_log WHERE outcome LIKE 'review_%' ORDER BY id DESC LIMIT 10`); the two kit pages printed.
- **The figures and their dates — re-check before teaching.** CGT 31% from 7 Oct 2026 (Budget 2027, 6 Oct 2026; the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026, 19 Oct 2026); the €1,270 exemption and the payment dates (Revenue, current); exit tax 38% in 2026, 35% from 1 Jan 2027 (the Bill); the Individual Investment Account from 1 Jul 2027 on a commencement order (the Bill: €12,000, €50,000, 1% on the average daily value above it, provider-calculated); stamp duty 1% / 0.5% / 0 (current); US withholding 15% with a W-8BEN (the treaty). The Bill becomes an Act in December if it passes: on the day it does, `TAX_RULES` in `src/lib/costs.ts` gets its `checkedOn` and `basis` updated and the page follows. The worksheet does not compute income tax on dividends, the four-week rule, a gain on the currency itself, or the connectivity cap; say so if asked.
- **The broker's figures** are as published in autumn 2026 (€1 + €1 on a US order; €2 on Euronext Dublin; €1 handling on core-selection ETFs; 0.25% AutoFX; €2.50 connectivity): confirm on the broker's site the week of the lesson and change `BROKER` if they moved. DEGIRO is the example because week 1 named it; it is not a recommendation, and the sentence to say is "any MiFID broker on an EU regulator's register works the same way; the numbers differ."
- **The review's budget:** `REVIEW_DAILY_CAP` (default 3) readings per account per 24 hours and `REVIEW_GLOBAL_CAP` (default 40) for the class, counted insert-first in `reviews` as the tutor's are — a failed call that returned nothing does not count, so an outage cannot spend a student's day; a reading costs about 1,500–2,500 input tokens and 300 output on Haiku. `"0"` on either switches the readings off and the audit stays. The prompt version (`r4` — r1 at the build, r2 after the review, r3 after VC-23: the next-week sentence once, in its own field; r4 the same day: F1 says the sheet carries no stop levels, closes or account figures by design, after a reading read the missing stop level as the student's omission) is on every row and on the page: if the prompt changes, the version changes, and an old reading says which prompt wrote it — and an old reading's fact tags open the facts of its own night, kept with it. **Watch the `review_rejected` receipts in the first weeks:** week 6's advice families read "sell" and "hold" as tips whatever the subject, and a Haiku sentence like "sell at the stop, as the rule says" would be withheld — the prompt steers it to "close" and "write the ticket"; if the receipts show it biting, a review-side allowance is the fix (OI-91). **The audit's calendar** is the universe's nights (the signal pass's dates), not the student's own report rows, so a night the risk pass skipped does not shorten a week; for a small cap visited every third day the stop's date is the close's, which can be two nights before the stamp — a short week for that tier once it is ticketable (OI-92).
- **"Nothing yet" accounts:** a student with no ticket gets the sentence and no reading — the rule has not opened for their instrument, or they waited; both are journal lines, and the review says so.
- **Asking the tutor about the account:** "account" is a loaded word in the notes (the paper account, the rules card) — ask about "the investment account the Bill would open" or "the Individual Investment Account", and the kit's passages come first.
- **Scripted answer — "Is the new account a good idea?":** "It is a set of rules about what the State charges on what you hold. The worksheet shows what they would charge on a paper trade; whether to use it is a decision — write it down, with the reason and the date, and re-check the rules when the Act is law."
- **Scripted answer — "Should I use DEGIRO?":** "The course named it in week 1 as the example of a cheap broker on a register. The habit is the register check and the fee page, for any broker; the choice is yours."
- **Scripted answer — "Why doesn't the review tell me whether I did well?":** "Because it can't see the result — on purpose. Eleven weeks ago we said we grade decisions, not outcomes; tonight the toolkit does it, and the only way to be sure it does is to hide the result from it."
- **Scripted answer — "What about crypto / forex platforms?":** "Outside the course's universe and the Bill's eligible assets; inside tonight's scam list. The register check applies before anything else does."
- **What the engine does not do, said out loud:** none of the toolkit's outcome figures on the sheet (your own words are — see 1:00); no size judged (the rule allows fewer shares, never more, so fewer is a choice, not a breach); no reading of minds — the journal notes are word tests, labelled as such; no tax computed on anyone's real affairs; no advice. The Central Bank's number and address in the scam segment were checked against the Bank's own warning notices at the build (a notice of 5 June 2026 carries both) — check again before printing.
- **The exit check (syllabus):** a review of a deliberately rule-breaking test portfolio flags the breach, not the P&L — pinned in `src/lib/review.test.ts` (the fixture: a stop open nine closes, a profitable early close, a stop answered within the week) and `src/review.test.ts` (a reading that praises the profit is withheld); the live check is **VC-23**: the test account's review on the live site, the audit's lines read against its tickets, one reading checked fact tag by fact tag, the `review_ok` receipt, and `/costs` on the live site with the defaults — the recipe in the shipment notes.

*Education, not advice. Nothing in this lesson or its toolkit is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything, to use any broker, or to arrange anyone's tax; the rates are as published on the dates given, a Bill is not an Act, and your own case is a question for Revenue's guidance or a tax adviser. A review grades process against the rules you chose; the result is not on its sheet.*
