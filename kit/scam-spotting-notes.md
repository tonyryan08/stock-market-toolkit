# Scam-spotting notes

Takeaway kit · Intro to the Stock Market · part 1 from lesson 6, part 2 from lesson 11

The course is designed to stop you losing money to bad process, high costs and scams — which, for most people who invest, is where the real money goes. Two pages of habits. None of them needs you to know anything about any company.

## Part 1 — Stories versus numbers (lesson 6)

1. **Every tip is a story.** Mr. Market "lets his enthusiasm or his fears run away with him" (Graham, ch. 8), and now he lives in your pocket. The defence is not cynicism but a habit: *every sentence about a company should point at a number on the record, and the number should be one you can find yourself.*
2. **"Guaranteed returns" is the whole tell.** A share's return is not known in advance by anyone; a 12% target is a target for the account's movement either way (week 8). Anyone who guarantees a number is describing a product that does not exist or one that is not a share.
3. **The pump.** A story, a chat group, a surge, a crowd of buyers, the insiders out. The course's rule reads the price (week 7) and sizes on the volatility (week 8); a share that jumped 40% on a story has a volatility the rule will size down, and a forecast capped at ±20 whatever the story says.
4. **The finfluencer.** A confident voice, a screenshot of a win, no mention of the losses, no number you can check. Ask: where is the record? Which number? Dated when? The course's explainer (week 6) and tutor (week 10) show their sources on every paragraph — *that* is the standard, and a human voice should meet it too.
5. **The confident wrong answer.** The better the prose, the more it is worth checking. A number from nowhere, a claim with no source, a reason that is really a feeling. The toolkit flags these in its own output; the habit is to do the same to everyone's.
6. **Opacity is the signal.** The course is built in the open — every rule, every figure, every check — because opacity is where the fee-takers and the scammers live. When you cannot see how a number was made, that is the finding.

## Part 2 — The platform itself (lesson 11)

**The four checks, before any money moves:**

1. **The register.** An Irish-authorised firm is on the Central Bank of Ireland's register (registers.centralbank.ie — "Check if a firm is regulated"); a firm serving Ireland from another EU country under the passport is on its home regulator's register. No register, no broker.
2. **The number from the register.** A **clone firm** copies a real firm's name, address and authorisation number and changes the bank account. The Bank publishes a warning about one most weeks. Call the number the register gives, not the one you were given.
3. **The fee page.** A real broker publishes one (lesson 11's walkthrough reads it line by line). A "platform" with no schedule, or a "manager" who takes a share of profits, is a different thing.
4. **The exit.** How do you get your money out, in what currency, in how many days, at what fee? If the answer is a story, stop.

**The red flags, one breath each:**

- An unregistered or "offshore-regulated" platform; a licence number you cannot find on a register.
- Guaranteed, "fixed" or "risk-free" returns; a return quoted per month.
- Pressure: a deadline, a bonus for depositing today, a call every day.
- An advert or a celebrity that found *you*; a message from a stranger; a "tip" in a group.
- Remote-access software "to help you set up"; being asked for your password or your card.
- A dashboard that only ever goes up; profits you can see but not withdraw; a fee to withdraw.
- A second firm that will "recover your losses" for a fee — the same people, a second time.
- Crypto or forex "platforms" with a manager; anything outside the course's universe of listed shares and funds on a regulated exchange.

**What you lose with an unauthorised firm:** the Investor Compensation Scheme (90% of a claim up to €20,000 against an authorised Irish firm) and the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman — both exist only for authorised firms. With an unauthorised one there is nobody to complain to and nothing to claim from.

**Report it:** the Central Bank of Ireland, (01) 224 5800; centralbank.ie/financialscams (the warnings list and the reporting form) — both as printed on the Bank's own warning notices (checked November 2026; the Bank's site is the source if either changes). Keep the messages, the account names, the dates.

**The link to the course:** a confident wrong answer and a confident wrong platform sell the same way — fluency. The defence is the same: find the register entry, find the number, check it yourself, write it down.

*Education, not advice. Nothing in this kit is a recommendation to use or avoid any particular firm; the habits are the point.*
