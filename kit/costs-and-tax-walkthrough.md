# Costs and tax — the walkthrough

Takeaway kit · Intro to the Stock Market · written for lesson 11, **as checked on 12 November 2026**

What a trade costs through a broker, and what the gain is taxed at in Ireland — the figures as published on the dates given, with the Bill marked as a Bill. This page is education: it says what the published rules would charge on a paper trade. It is not tax advice, and your own case is a question for Revenue's guidance or a tax adviser. The toolkit's worksheet (`/costs`) does the arithmetic on any numbers you give it.

## 1 · Before any money moves

- **Find the firm on a regulator's register.** An Irish-authorised firm is on the Central Bank of Ireland's register (registers.centralbank.ie — "Check if a firm is regulated"); a firm serving Ireland from another EU country under the passport is on its home regulator's register (DEGIRO: BaFin in Germany, with a Dutch branch under the AFM). A firm on no register is not a broker.
- **Use the register's contact details**, not the ones on the website or in the message that found you. A clone firm copies a real firm's name, address and authorisation number and changes only the bank account.
- **The profile.** A basic account — no margin, no short selling, no derivatives — is the only kind the course recognises: the Starter System's unleveraged variant.
- **The forms.** A **W-8BEN** (the US tax form a non-US investor signs through the broker) brings the withholding on US dividends from 30% to 15% under the Ireland–US treaty; the first dividend statement shows which rate you got.
- **The small print.** Securities lending (may the broker lend your shares, and for what?); the compensation scheme (which country's, and how much — DEGIRO's is German: deposits to €100,000, investments 90% to €20,000); the connectivity fee for a foreign exchange.

## 2 · An order, in the course's words

## 3 · What a trade costs

The worksheet's defaults — €10,000 into a US share through a euro account, sold three years later with a 30% gain — on a published Irish schedule (DEGIRO, autumn 2026; confirm on the broker's site):

| Line | Euro | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Commission and handling, buy | 2.00 | €1 + €1 an order |
| Commission and handling, sell | 2.00 | |
| Currency conversion, buy | 25.00 | 0.25% of €10,000 (Auto FX; a manual conversion is a fixed fee plus 0.25%) |
| Currency conversion, sell | 32.50 | 0.25% of the €13,000 sale |
| The spread | 10.00 | 0.1% of the value for a large cap — a small cap's can be ten times that |
| Exchange connectivity | 7.50 | €2.50 a year for a foreign exchange, three years |
| **To open and close** | **79.00** | the course's model ($2 + 0.25% a ticket, both tickets) says $61.50 |

A euro-listed UCITS ETF in a broker's core selection: €1 an order, no conversion, no stamp duty — and an **annual charge** (TER) taken inside the price: 0.2% a year is €69 over the three years on the average value, the cost you never see on a statement.

**Stamp duty** on buying shares: 1% for an Irish-registered company, 0.5% for a UK one, none on US shares or Irish-domiciled ETFs.

The sentence: *the trade costs tens of euro; the holding costs tens a year; the tax costs hundreds.*

## 4 · The same gain taxed three ways

The worksheet's €3,000 gain (€10,000 → €13,000 over three years):

| | Shares, outside any account | A fund or ETF, outside | Inside the Individual Investment Account |
|---|---|---|---|
| The tax | **Capital gains tax** | **The exit tax** | **A 1% charge on value** |
| The rate | 31% for disposals on or after 7 Oct 2026 (33% before) — *Budget 2027; the Bill* | 38% in 2026; **35% from 1 Jan 2027** (41% to the end of 2025) — *the Bill* | 1% a year on the average daily value above €50,000; nothing below it — *the Bill* |
| Exemption | the first €1,270 of a year's gains | none | — |

Losses, the years in between, and dividends:

| | Shares, outside | A fund or ETF, outside | Inside the account |
|---|---|---|---|
| Losses | offset gains on shares; carry forward (the four-week rule limits a loss on shares bought back within four weeks) | no relief: a fund's loss cannot be set against anything | the charge is payable in a losing year too |
| While you hold | nothing | a **deemed disposal every eight years**: the gain taxed as if sold, credited at the real sale | nothing |
| Dividends | income (section 5) | taxed at the exit-tax rate | no Irish tax; US withholding — for the Act to settle |

What it comes to on the worksheet's gain, when it is paid, and what is law tonight:

| | Shares, outside | A fund or ETF, outside | Inside the account |
|---|---|---|---|
| On €3,000 | **€536.30** (31% of €1,730) | **€1,050** (35% of €3,000; €1,140 if sold in 2026) | **€0** (the balance never passes €50,000) |
| When and how | pay by **15 December** (disposals Jan–Nov) or **31 January** (December's); return by **31 October** of the following year on CG1 or Form 11 / Form 12 | an ETF (held through a clearing system) and an EU fund: self-assessed on Form 11 / Form 12 by 31 October of the following year, for a sale or an eighth anniversary; an Irish non-ETF fund deducts the tax itself | the provider calculates it each 31 December and deducts it by 14 April; you file nothing |
| Status tonight | the rate is a Bill (announced, drafted, not yet an Act); the exemption and the dates are current law | the 35% is a Bill; the 38% is law | the whole account is a Bill, to open from **1 July 2027** on a commencement order |

**The Individual Investment Account** (the Bill's name; "Personal Investment Account" in the Budget speech): 18 or over, Irish-resident, one account per person; contributions of up to **€12,000** a year, no minimum, no lock-in; eligible assets are listed shares and bonds, UCITS funds and ETFs, and insurance products; transfers between providers; providers register with Revenue. Two things the Bill as published does not settle: whether a share listed only in the US qualifies as an eligible asset, and how US withholding is treated inside. Both are for the Act and Revenue's guidance. A €10,000 start fits one year's allowance; a €50,000 pot takes at least five years of contributions.

## 5 · Dividends

- A **US** dividend: 30% withheld at source, **15%** with a W-8BEN on file; then income in Ireland — taxed at your marginal rate with USC and PRSI, with credit for the 15%.
- An **Irish** company's dividend: 25% dividend withholding tax taken, credited against the income tax due.
- A **fund's** distribution: the exit-tax rate (38% in 2026; 35% from 2027 — *the Bill*), no USC or PRSI on top.
- The worksheet shows the withholding and stops: the rest depends on the person.

## 6 · The record you keep

For every buy and sell: the date, the quantity, the price, the currency conversion rate, the cost line — and the reason. The broker's annual statement is raw data (it reports nothing to Revenue for you); the ticket has carried all of this since week 8, and a journal entry is a tax record with a reason attached. Keep contract notes; keep the dividend statements (they show the withholding); keep the eighth anniversaries of any fund in a diary.

## 8 · The discipline

Every figure above carries a date. A Bill is not an Act: before anyone acts on a figure marked "the Bill", it is re-checked against the Finance Act and Revenue's published guidance. The toolkit's worksheet prints the date it was checked on and marks every line that depends on the Bill; the course updates it when the law changes. That is the habit — not the numbers.
