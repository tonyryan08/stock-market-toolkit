# Reading List (seeded week 1 — grows with the course)

**Takeaway kit · Intro to the Stock Market**

Nothing here is required in week 1. Assigned chapters are announced in the week they matter; everything else is for curiosity. Your library can source most of these — the good ideas in investing are old enough to be free.

## The two books the course is built on

- **Benjamin Graham, *The Intelligent Investor*** — chapters 8 (Mr. Market), 14 (stock selection for the defensive investor) and 20 (margin of safety), assigned across weeks 4–6. The rest is optional; those three chapters are the value half of this course, and chapter 14 is where our screener's criteria actually come from.
- **Benjamin Graham & David Dodd, *Security Analysis*** — the professional companion, and the older, heavier book (1934). Not assigned: nobody needs 700 pages of Depression-era prose to pass this course. But it is where the *reasoning* beneath the checklist lives — why book value matters, why earnings must be examined over a full cycle, what a margin of safety is actually protecting you from. Recommended for anyone who finishes week 5 wanting to argue with the criteria rather than just apply them.
- **Robert Carver, *Leveraged Trading*** — the Starter System chapters, assigned across weeks 7–9. We use his rules on ordinary shares with no leverage; the system's discipline is the point, not the leverage.

## Rules, the Carver way (Phase 3, weeks 7–9)

- **Robert Carver, *Systematic Trading*** (2015) — the fuller book behind the Starter System: chapter 1 on the flawed human brain, chapter 3 on fooling yourself by fitting rules to the past, chapter 7 on forecasts (the rule the toolkit runs, and why the scalar is 3.75). Optional; the tutor's course copy is this one.
- **pysystemtrade** — Carver's own open-source trading system (github.com/robcarver17/pysystemtrade, GPL). The toolkit's constants were checked against it; you can read the same rule in his hand. For the curious, not required.
- **Brad Barber & Terrance Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth"** — *Journal of Finance* 55(2), 2000. The 66,000-household study behind week 7's first slide. Free versions on the authors' pages.
- **Terrance Odean, "Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?"** — *Journal of Finance* 53(5), 1998. The disposition effect, measured.
- **Brian Hurst, Yao Hua Ooi & Lasse Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing"** — *Journal of Portfolio Management* 44(1), 2017. Trend rules like ours, 1880–2016, 67 markets: modest returns, long drawdowns. Free on AQR's site. The honest answer to "does this actually make money?".

## On luck, skill, and judging decisions (the week-1 theme)

- **Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets*** — where "resulting" comes from; short and readable.
- **Michael Mauboussin, *The Success Equation*** — the luck–skill continuum, properly worked out.
- **Morgan Housel, *The Psychology of Money*** — behaviour beats spreadsheets; the friendliest book on this list.

## Sensible general grounding

- **John Kay, *The Long and the Short of It*** — investing for "people who are not in the industry," UK/Ireland-flavoured and refreshingly unexcitable.

## The weekly press habit (optional but recommended)

- ***Investors' Chronicle*** — weekly, aimed at private investors; it still runs quantitative stock screens, including Graham-style ones, which will look very familiar by week 5. (investorschronicle.co.uk)
- ***The Financial Times*** and ***The Economist*** — for the world the companies live in. Paywalled; many Irish libraries carry both in print or via their digital services — ask at the desk before subscribing to anything.

## Free lectures

- **LSE public events** — the London School of Economics runs free public lectures, streamed and archived as video and podcast (lse.ac.uk/events). One curated event per phase is part of the course; the term calendar and podcast feed links will be added here when published.

## Scam literacy (weeks 6 and 11, but bookmark now)

- **CCPC** — the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission's scam guidance (ccpc.ie).
- **Central Bank of Ireland** — check any firm against the register, and read the warnings list, *before* money moves (centralbank.ie).
