# Student Quickstart

**Takeaway kit · Intro to the Stock Market**

## What you need for this course

1. **A browser.** Phone, tablet or laptop — all fine.
2. **The course web address**, written on the whiteboard each week.
3. **Your decision journal** (printed) and a pen.
4. From week 12, **the email address you gave the tutor** — the site sends a sign-in code to it.

That's the entire kit list. **You will never be asked to install anything.**

## Each week

Open the course address. The site grows a new capability every week, in step with the lesson — week 1 is one page and one button; by week 9 you'll have a paper portfolio, a value screener and a risk report sitting there; from week 10 a tutor you can question; from week 11 a review of your own process and a worksheet of what the real world charges. Nothing you do on it costs money or touches a real market. **Paper portfolios only, all twelve weeks.**

## Ask the tutor (from week 10)

`/tutor` on the course address. It has read the course's notes — the lesson plans and this kit — and nothing else: not the internet, not the news, not your account. Ask in the course's words ("how is the stop calculated?", "what is a drawdown?"), read the answer, and **check the citations**: every paragraph names the passages it rests on, and the passages are on the page below the answer — find the sentence, find the number. A number that is in no passage is flagged; a tip is withheld (the course does not say what to buy, and neither does its tutor). It cannot see your account or tonight's prices — your page and `/signals` have those. No code is asked for; the class can see what was asked, not who asked.

`/watchlist` is the two rules read side by side: Graham's screens (*what*) and Carver's rule (*when*, *how much*) as a gate. Most nights it says "nothing" — an empty gate is a result, and "waiting" is a decision worth a journal line.

## Did you follow your rules? (from week 11)

On your own page, "review my process": every ticket and position read against the rules you chose. The **audit** needs no AI — a stop hit and the position still open, a close with the stop not hit, a journal entry that names no rule — and is always there. The **reading** is written by the course's AI from a sheet of process facts that carries no profit, loss or account value, so it can only talk about process; a reading that praises a result, invents a number, cites a fact that is not there, or tips is withheld, and the page says why. Three readings a day per account; the button lands you back on the page with the reading. Check the fact tags, as with the tutor — the sheet carries your own journal words as you wrote them, so a result you wrote yourself is the one thing it can see.

`/costs` is the worksheet from lesson 11: what a trade really costs through a broker, in euro, and the same gain taxed three ways — as shares, as a fund or ETF, and inside the investment account the Finance Bill would open in July 2027 — every rate with its date, the Bill marked as a Bill. The kit's walkthrough (`costs-and-tax-walkthrough.md`) and the scam-spotting notes go with it.

## Signing in, and your page (from week 12)

From week 12 the site stands behind a door: open the course address and Cloudflare asks for your email, sends a one-time code to it, and lets you in for a day or so before asking again. Only the addresses the tutor enrolled are on the list — a friend with the address gets the door and no further. Once inside, **`/me` is your page**: the site finds your paper account by the email you signed in with, so there is no address to keep secret any more. The old `/tickets/<code>` address still works, and it opens for you and for the tutor only; the review page and the worksheet are where they were. If `/me` says no account is linked to your email, tell the tutor — the enrolment list carries the link.

The sign-in is Cloudflare's, not the course's: the toolkit never sees your code and stores no password. It is handed your email and matches it to your account, and that is all it does with it.

## Wait — don't I need to set up the software?

No. Your tutor builds the toolkit; you use it. Building it is a separate craft that happens off-stage so that class time stays on the actual subject: how to make good investment decisions and how to avoid being parted from your money.

## Then why is the code public?

Because this course tells you that opacity is where the fee-takers and the scammers live, and it would be poor form to then ask you to trust a black box. Every line of the toolkit — every rule in the screener, every number in the risk calculation — is readable by anyone, forever, at the repository address in your kit. **You don't need a GitHub account to read it.** Click the link, browse the files, same as any web page.

If you're curious enough to want to run it yourself, say so and your tutor will point you at the builder's guide. It's genuinely optional, and it is not part of the course.

## The three promises, so you can hold us to them

- **Paper portfolios only.** Nobody risks a cent in this room.
- **No tips, ever.** You leave with a process, not a portfolio.
- **Everything is open.** Every tool we use is built in public.
